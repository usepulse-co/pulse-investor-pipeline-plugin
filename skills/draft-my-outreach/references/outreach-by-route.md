# Outreach by route

Three shapes. Pick the one for the firm's route. Every draft uses the founder's confirmed profile and traction line and nothing else.

## Cold email (a pitch address or a general address on the firm's site)

Under 120 words. Subject on its own line. Plain text, no formatting the founder has to strip, no attachment unless the firm's site asks for one.

The person reading a pitch inbox opens the subject, reads the first two lines, and decides whether to open the deck. The email has one job: get the deck opened. So the subject is the company, what it does in five or six words, and the round. Add the headline number when it is strong; associates triage on subjects with a number in them.

```
Subject: Acme, payroll for construction subcontractors, 2.5M seed, 180k ARR

Hi,

Acme runs payroll for construction subcontractors. Crews are paid weekly in the field, pay is often late, and the tools built for office payroll do not handle it.

We are at 180k ARR with 40 customers, up from 12 in January. We are raising 2 to 3M to add two states and a sales hire. Your site says you lead seed rounds in fintech, which is why I am writing.

Deck: <link>

Would you take 20 minutes next week?

Sam Lee, CEO, Acme
<phone>
```

In order: what the company does and for whom, with the problem in one concrete sentence; traction and the raise; the deck link; one ask with a time frame; name, role, company. The firm reason is one short clause, and only when it is specific.

**When to mention the firm at all.** The list's reasons are mostly "backs fintech and B2B software at seed", which every firm on the list shares and which the reader already knows about their own fund. A line like that is filler and marks the email as templated. Use a firm reason only when it is specific: a phrase quoted from their own thesis page ("you say you look for software for under-digitised industries"), or a portfolio company the founder knows is adjacent. Never a filing, never a fund size, never "I see you recently raised". When there is nothing specific, leave the firm out and let the fit show through the description.

**Greeting.** "Hi," with no name is fine for a pitch inbox and better than a guessed name. If the founder found a partner on the team page who clearly covers their space, "Hi Jane,". Never "Dear Sir or Madam", never "Hi team".

**General inbox** (`hello@`, `info@`). The reader is often an assistant or an ops person who forwards. Open with the forward request, keep the rest identical, without claiming a comparative reply rate:

```
Hi,

Could you pass this to whoever looks at seed fintech?

Acme runs payroll for construction subcontractors...
```

**Pitch inbox** (`pitch@`, `deals@`, `apply@`). No forward request. Straight into the company.

## Application form

Lay out the form's questions in order. Under each, the answer, trimmed to the character limit when the founder gives it. Mark what only the founder can do.

```
Firm B application form: <url>

1. Company name
Acme

2. One sentence on what you do (140 characters)
Payroll for construction subcontractors, built for crews paid weekly in the field.

3. What problem do you solve and for whom? (500 characters)
Subcontractors run 5 to 50 person crews paid weekly on site. Office payroll tools assume salaried staff and a desk; a late run means crews walk to the next job. Acme runs the weekly cycle from a phone, handles the per-job splits, and files the state paperwork.

4. Traction
180k ARR, 40 customers, up from 12 in January 2026.

5. How much are you raising and at what stage?
2 to 3M seed.

6. Deck
You fill this: paste <link> or upload the PDF, whichever the field takes.

7. How did you hear about us?
Your website.
```

When the form's fields are not known, use this standard set and say the form may differ: company name, one-liner, problem and who has it, product, traction, team, raise amount and stage, location, deck, how you heard of us.

Rules for the answers:

- Each field answers its own question. The problem field is where the reader slows down; it gets the concrete sentence, not the one-liner again.
- Same voice as the email: specific, in the founder's units, no adjectives doing the work of numbers.
- When no limit is known, keep each answer under about 400 characters. Most forms cap there and the founder should not have to cut on the page.
- "How did you hear about us" gets the truth. "Your website" or "a list of seed fintech investors" is fine; a made-up referral is not.
- Whether the deck field takes a link or an upload is not recorded yet. Say so once and let the founder see on the page. A tracked link only helps where the form takes a link.

## Forwardable blurb (intro-only firms)

Two parts. The ask is for the person who might know the firm; the blurb is what that person pastes to the investor, so it is in the third person, has no greeting and no sign-off, and says nothing about the forwarder or about why the founder is asking around.

```
Ask (to the contact):
Do you know anyone at Firm C? They only take referred deals and they back exactly our kind of company. If you do, here is a blurb you can forward as is. No pressure if not.

Blurb:
Acme runs payroll for construction subcontractors, whose crews are paid weekly on site and walk when pay is late. They are at 180k ARR with 40 customers, up from 12 in January, and raising a 2 to 3M seed. Sam Lee (CEO) would like 20 minutes. Deck: <link>
```

Then ask the founder: "Who do you know who knows Firm C?" Nothing more. If they name someone, address the ask to that person. The blurb stays the same for every contact; only the ask changes.

## Follow-up (when the founder asks)

Same thread, shorter, with a confirmed new fact when there is one. Without an update, write a short reminder and ask; never invent a milestone. No apology or "just checking in".

```
Subject: Re: Acme, payroll for construction subcontractors, 2.5M seed, 180k ARR

Hi,

One update since I wrote: we signed our first general contractor, which brings 11 of their subcontractors on in one go.

Still keen to get your view. 20 minutes next week?

Sam
```

A further follow-up can be two lines: a confirmed new fact and the ask. Keep the ledger unchanged until the founder reports sending or a response; only mark "no reply" when they report that outcome. A silent firm is not a no; it can be written to again when the round has a lead, with that as the new fact.

Keep forms on the form route. A published email does not justify duplicate outreach through another channel. Use any published submission-status mechanism, or leave a reminder on the founder's agreed date.

## Things never to write

- A relationship or referral the founder did not state
- A metric the founder did not confirm
- A person's name that the founder did not find on the firm's own site
- A filing date, a fund size, or "I see you recently closed a fund"
- "I hope this finds you well", "I would love to", "quick question", "circling back", "just checking in"
- A paragraph about the firm
- Anything about Pulse pricing or plans
- Em dashes
